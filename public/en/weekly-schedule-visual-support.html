Weekly visual schedule for kids

A weekly visual schedule helps a child see what kind of day it is, not only what is happening this minute. It is useful when weekdays and weekends differ, when siblings have different clubs, or when “what happens tomorrow?” needs an answer before bedtime.

Daily schedule and weekly schedule

A daily schedule shows today, step by step. A weekly schedule shows how the days differ — Monday with football, Wednesday at the other parent’s house, Friday with a film. Pictures make that difference visible for children who are not reading a calendar yet.

In My Starday you can use both: the week for the overview, and the day for morning and bedtime detail. Start from the visual schedule guide if the idea is new.

When a weekly view helps

  • School and after-school. Different activities on different days.
  • Two homes. The child can see which home a day belongs to.
  • Siblings on different timetables. Each child gets their own overview.
  • “What happens tomorrow?” The week answers it in advance.

How to build one

  1. List what actually changes. School, training, childcare, the one-off trip.
  2. Pick one picture per kind of day or per important activity.
  3. Attach a daily routine to each weekday. The same morning Monday to Friday, a different one on Saturday.
  4. Preview it together. Sunday evening is a good moment to look at the week ahead.

Example: a school week

  • Monday–Friday: morning routine, school, homework after school
  • Tuesday: swimming after school
  • Wednesday: the other home (a different morning template)
  • Saturday: a later morning
  • Sunday: a quiet look at the week ahead

Pair it with a reward chart

The week answers which day it is. A reward chart supports the child in actually doing the steps. Together they give a shape to the week and a reason to follow it. Read morning routines for the daily half.

Paper or app

You can print a weekly schedule PDF from the resource library. The app is the living version: a moved swimming lesson does not mean reprinting the whole week. The child sees today in their own view. You plan in the parent view.

Questions parents ask

Daily means the steps for today. Weekly means how the days differ. Many families want both.
It often starts to matter around school age, when the week varies more. Younger children usually do better with today’s schedule first.
